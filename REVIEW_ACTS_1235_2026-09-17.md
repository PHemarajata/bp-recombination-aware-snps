## The finding: synthesis is not deterministic

Identical text, same voice, same model, same speed, two runs:

| line | run 1 | run 2 | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 line 1 | 6.64 s | 7.62 s | **+14.7%** |
| Act 1 line 3 | 4.46 s | 5.15 s | **+15.6%** |
| Act 1 line 4 | 4.04 s | 4.69 s | **+16.1%** |
| Act 2 line 2 | 5.20 s | 4.50 s | **-13.4%** |
| Act 1 line 2 | 8.78 s | 8.36 s | -4.8% |
| Act 2 line 6 | 7.01 s | 6.87 s | -2.0% |
| Act 1 line 5 | 7.99 s | 8.03 s | +0.6% |
| Act 2 line 3 | 3.44 s | 3.44 s | 0.0% |

**Up to sixteen percent on the same words.** This was found by accident: a copy
failed silently because its destination did not exist yet, so every line was
re-synthesized and the fit table changed under a script that had not been edited.

Three consequences, and they apply to every act from here:

1. **The audio file is the artifact, not the text.** A line that fits is a fact
   about a file, not about a sentence.
2. **Never re-generate a line that fits.** Re-anchoring is free; re-generating
   rolls the dice again.
3. **The fit check must run against the exact files that will be muxed.** A fit
   measured on a previous run's audio is not evidence about this run's.

This is why `NARRATION_ACT4_2026-09-17.md` says regenerate rather than re-time when
that act moves to the TUC series. That instruction was right for the wrong reason:
the model and speed differ there, but even at identical settings the durations
would not have carried.
