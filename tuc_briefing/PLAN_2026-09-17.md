Written 2026-09-17 after reviewing the clip 2 and clip 3 pictures. Both came
back with the same structural defect, which means the cause is upstream of
either builder and will recur on clip 4 unless it is fixed first.

## The one thing to fix before anything else

**`SHORTLIST.md` still specifies the wrong voice and the wrong rate, and every
brief's per-act seconds are computed from it.** It records the voice as settled
on `eleven_multilingual_v2` at 168.0 wpm and says "every budget in these briefs
is stated at that rate".

Neither half survived contact with clip 1. Clip 1 was voiced on **`eleven_v3`**,
and the 168.0 figure was refit downward afterwards from clip 1's own audio,
because spelled-out numbers cost far more time per word than the measurement
passage contained. Both delivered NOTEs cite v2 at 168 wpm, and both builders
then padded to the seconds that rate produces.

This is the root cause of the padding on both clips, and clip 4 will inherit it.

**Replace the per-act second counts with a density target taken from the only
finished clip in the series.** Clip 1 carries 611 words over 291.02 s, which is
**126 words per minute of runtime**. Against that:

| | runtime of new acts | brief's words | words at 126 wpm | over by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clip 2 | 210 s | 475 | 441 | 34 words, 8% |
| clip 3 | 237 s | 535 | 498 | 37 words, 7% |

So the runtimes are close to right and the word budgets are slightly hot. That
is a much smaller problem than it looked, and it points the fix away from
re-cutting the films.

## The structural problem, and the three ways to go

Both pictures stop moving long before the words run out. Measured on the
delivered files, as the share of runtime sitting in a hold of three seconds or
more, where nothing new appears:

| | hold share |
|---|---|
| clip 3 new acts | 81% to 99% |
| clip 2 new acts | 52% to 78% |
| `TreeBuilderPaired`, reused | 75% |
| `RecursiveSubdivision`, reused | **22%** |

`RecursiveSubdivision` is the standard to aim at, and it is already in the
series, built in the same house style. It shows the target is reachable.

**Option A, add animated steps inside the existing runtimes. Recommended.**
Keeps every brief's content, keeps the runtimes, keeps the reused clips looking
of a piece with the new ones. The work is confined to the held stretches, which
the reviews list by timestamp. Both clips also leave the lower third of the
frame empty while they hold, so there is somewhere to put the new steps.

**Option B, cut the acts down to the motion that is actually there.** Cheapest
to execute and worst for the deck: it would take clip 3's new acts from 237 s to
roughly 40 s and throw away most of the brief's argument. Not recommended.

**Option C, accept it as a narrated slide deck and stop calling it a film.**
Defensible for a technical briefing, and the frames are good frames. But the two
reused clips are genuinely animated, so they will read as borrowed from
somewhere else, and the series premise is that motion carries the ideas that a
still cannot.

I recommend A, with a measurable gate so it can be checked rather than argued:
**no more than 40% of an act's runtime in holds of three seconds or more**, and
no single hold over about 4 s. Both are one command to verify on the delivered
file, which is the point.

## Why the builders' own sign-off cannot be relied on

Clip 2's NOTE claimed every figure carried its denominator and that the recovery
share and the false positive rate never shared a frame. Act 6 puts both in one
frame with neither denominator. Clip 3's NOTE claimed zero text overlaps across
all sixty holds. Act 3 holds its conclusion over the measured unit marks for
most of the act.

Both harnesses inspect the scene graph at animation boundaries, so they check
what was *asked for* rather than what was *rendered*, and neither measures how
long anything sits still. Clip 3's NOTE is otherwise excellent and its numbers
are all correct, so this is a gap in the instrument, not bad faith.

**Ask the builder to ship the measurements, not the assurances**: the per-act
hold table and the frame audit, run on its own delivered mp4 files, pasted into
the NOTE. That converts the claim into something a reviewer can check in
seconds.

## A fourth data problem, found while doing the other three

**`NUMBERS.tsv` is not reproducible, and running the script that maintains it
would have deleted most of what the clips quote.**

The file holds 101 figures. `generate_numbers.py` builds 66 of them and wrote
the file with a plain truncating open, so the other **35 were carried by hand
and would vanish the next time anyone ran it** -- including every `controls.*`
constant and the six `rm.*` keys holding the Gate 1 window, its brackets, the
in-window IQR and the floor sensitivity. That is very nearly the whole numeric
basis of clips 2 and 3, plus the three `treebuilder.*` values clip 3 hands to
its reused act 5. The script's own docstring says to run it "before quoting
anything, and immediately before submission", and the file is untracked and
gitignored, so those 35 figures existed in exactly one place on one machine with
a standing instruction to destroy them.

Fixed in this pass: the write now merges, so computed keys refresh in place,
carried keys keep their position, and every carried key is named on stdout. The
merge is unit-tested, including a control confirming the old write would have
dropped figures. The 35 carried figures are still not computed, which is the
real remaining debt, but they can no longer be lost by accident.

## Data fixes to land before the revision renders

These are upstream of the renders, so doing them first avoids re-drawing the
same defect twice. Items 1 to 3 are done; see "Done in this pass" below.

1. **The 19-of-21 pairing.** `SPIKEIN_RESULT.txt` prints 0.91 and 0.90 from
   identical inputs because the counts are pooled across replicates while the
   rate is a mean of per-replicate rates. The pairing propagated to
   `NUMBERS.tsv`, `TABLES.md` Table 4, `BRIEF-02` and clip 2's act 5, which
   draws the same fraction at two different bar heights. Decide whether the
   published figure is 90% (the pooled fraction) or 91% (the mean of three
   replicates), then make every copy say the same thing. Clip 2's revision
   redraws this bar, so fix it before that render.
2. **Register act 7's three numbers.** 34 of 47 in-window units, ten falling
   under the seven-member floor, and a funded median of 7.7394 against 7.6988
   all reproduce exactly, but they are recorded nowhere. The definition is
   `IP-`/`IE-` sample identifiers joined to `GATE1_ALIGNMENT_2026-08-21.tsv`.
   `SHORTLIST.md` says the funded isolates get "totaled in clip 4", so clip 4
   will need them and should not have to re-derive the definition.
3. **Decide on the word "funded".** Clip 1's narration dropped the funding
   framing deliberately; clip 3's act 7 puts "funded units" back on screen
   beside its own better phrase, "isolates this project sequenced". Pick one and
   put it in the shortlist so clip 4 inherits it.

## Suggested order

Doc and data first because everything else depends on it, then the two pictures
in parallel since they touch different files, then narration, then clip 4.

1. **Shortlist and data fixes.** The voice block, the density target, the
   acceptance gate, the three items above. Documentation and one TSV; no
   rendering.
2. **Clip 2 revision.** Four blockers: two label fixes in act 5 and act 6, one
   coordinate change in act 1, and added steps in the tails.
3. **Clip 3 revision.** Four blockers: act 3's relayout and its source note,
   act 7's readout, and added steps. Act 3 is the only real design work.
4. **Narrate clips 2 and 3** against the frozen pictures, on `eleven_v3` with a
   fixed seed, to about 126 words per minute of runtime.
5. **Clip 4.** The longest at about eight minutes, and the one carrying the
   attribution content, so it should go last and be fed to the builder after
   clips 2 and 3 have passed. The brief was edited today and should be settled
   before it goes out.
6. **Assemble and deliver**, with subtitles toggleable, and a silent build kept
   in case the chief is presented to live rather than watching alone.

## Two things worth deciding now

- **Is 126 words per minute of runtime the budget, or is clip 1 itself a bit

- **Does clip 4 get briefed before or after the revisions land?** Briefing it
