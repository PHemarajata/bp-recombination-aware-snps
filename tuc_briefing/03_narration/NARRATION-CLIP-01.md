## TucC1Act1

1. Three hundred twenty six isolates were sequenced, and three hundred twelve were confirmed B. pseudomallei.
2. Two hundred fifty nine from patients, fifty three from the environment.
3. Three findings carried forward. The local population is extraordinarily diverse, with no dominant type.
4. Clinical and environmental isolates are drawn from one population.
5. A shared sequence type is not enough to claim a link, so attribution needs core-genome distance.
6. All of it from one province. Nakhon Phanom.

## TucC1Act2

1. Emerging resistance was one of the things this sequencing was meant to settle.
2. No acquired resistance genes were detected.
3. What is present is intrinsic to the species.
4. Two hundred nineteen of three hundred twelve carry no determinant at all. Seventy percent.
5. First line therapy is unaffected. Ceftazidime and trimethoprim, two hundred fifty three susceptible of two hundred fifty eight tested.
6. That leaves one loose end, already scheduled. Eleven isolates were non-susceptible with no genomic cause.
7. They get repeat testing, with MIC determination, in the next phase.
8. Two isolates carry the only quinolone determinant found.
9. The province has a baseline now, so a future change is visible against it.
10. Resistance: asked, answered, and set aside. The rest of this series is about a different question.

## TucC1Act3

1. These genomes have been placed in a global panel twice.

4. The panel was expanded to cover more countries, and the clustering was refined.
5. Of those, two thousand three hundred forty enter analysis.

## TucC1Act4

1. The partition already exists, built from two thousand three hundred forty genomes.
2. Now drop the three hundred twelve into it, and watch where they land.
3. Two hundred seventy six enter the analysed set.
4. They land in fifty six of the eighty five units.
5. Not a cluster, and not a corner of the tree.
6. A provincial collection reaching across a global panel is what lets it answer questions no single province could.
7. Thirty six did not enter.
8. Every one has a nearest unit recorded, so none of them is lost.
9. Their lineages are too rare in the panel for a measurable unit to form.
10. That is a finding about the panel, not a defect in the isolates.

## TucC1Act5

1. The closeout found environmental isolates scattered among patient isolates.
2. There was no separate environmental lineage. But that was one tree, built from these three hundred twelve genomes alone.
3. So test it again in a structure that knew nothing about which isolate came from where.
4. Twenty of the eighty five units hold both a patient isolate and an environmental one.
5. A unit is coarse. Sharing one means one population, not a link from patient to source.
6. Thirty four of the forty two environmental isolates sit in one of those mixed units.
7. Sixteen of those twenty units sit inside the measurable range.
8. Same answer as the closeout, at a different scale: one population.

## TucC1Act6

1. Here is the problem this now makes it possible to ask.
2. A patient presents with severe sepsis and no travel history at all.
3. No exposure, no source. The genome is the only evidence of where it came from.
4. The closeout already settled two things about that.
5. Two isolates can share a sequence type, the same broad genetic family.
6. Measured across the whole genome, they can still be far apart.
7. So sharing a sequence type is not enough to answer the question.
8. It needs distance measured across the whole core genome, not a handful of loci.
9. So with core genomes, in a global frame, how far can you get?

## TucC1Act7

1. Answering that question takes three things, in order.
2. First, the measuring instrument has to be shown to work.
3. Then it has to be pointed at the data.
4. Then the answer, and just as important, the point where it stops working.
