## Act 1. Why the obvious approach fails

1. Start with the process, not the measurement.
2. In this species DNA also moves between lineages.
3. So a difference has two possible origins. Mutation accumulated over time,
4. or a block of DNA that arrived from another lineage.
5. The ratio between those two contributions is r over m.
6. It is the one quantity everything downstream depends on.
7. Now the trap. You can compute one r over m for the whole species.
8. It will return a number, and the number is meaningless.
9. One number averages across all that structure, and hides it.
10. It is not shown here. Clip three shows it where it belongs.

## Act 2. Partition first, because the biology says so

1. The reason is a barrier that exists in the biology.
2. Consider two clades of this organism.
3. What follows is argued from the literature, not measured in this study.
4. Restriction-modification systems differ between them.
5. They restrict which DNA a cell will accept, so a crossing block is stopped.
6. It never gets the chance to recombine, so clades behave as functional units of isolation.
7. The evidence is a hundred and six strains in one Asian locale.
8. Whether it holds globally has not been tested.
9. Averaging across a real barrier is not a shortcut.
10. Partitioning first is a biological requirement, not a convenience.
11. The axis is unit diversity, in mean pairwise core SNPs.
12. Every unit in the next two clips sits somewhere on it.
13. So measure r over m within each unit.

## Act 3 lead-in. How the detector actually works

1. The detector scans for a local excess of SNP density.
2. Whether that excess is visible depends entirely on the background it sits against.
3. So the same block is obvious in one unit and invisible in another.

## Act 3. The detection window (reused render)

1. The detector looks for a local excess of SNP density.
2. One imported piece sits in this stretch, and it never changes.
3. The bars show what counts as recombination, and what as mutation.
4. Only the background changes. Here is the working window.

10. Too similar and too different both read low.
11. Only inside the window does it mean anything.

## Act 4 lead-in. Does it invent recombination?

1. Before trusting it, the detector has to be shown finding nothing where there is nothing.
2. The identical pipeline on zero-recombination simulations.
3. Fifteen hundred nineteen replicates.
4. Twenty returned any call at all.

## Act 4. The negative control (reused render)

1. Every laboratory runs a no-template control.
2. We ran the same idea: genomes simulated with zero recombination, identical pipeline.
3. Fifteen hundred nineteen replicates over sixty two unit replicons.
4. Almost nothing came back.
5. The largest value in the whole run was vanishingly small.
6. Now watch the scale. It stays linear the whole way.
7. It widens until the real analysis units appear.
8. These are real units from the collection.
9. Everything the null produced sits below seven thousandths.
10. A separation of four hundred fold and more.
11. So the tool is not manufacturing recombination.

## Act 5. Does it find what is really there?

1. The other half of the control asks the opposite question.
2. Implant recombination tracts of known length, taken from donors of known divergence.
3. The axis is the share that comes back.
4. Two point four SNPs: four of twenty.
5. Four point two SNPs: eight of twenty.
6. Nine SNPs: nineteen of twenty one, ninety one percent.
7. Twenty five SNPs, then forty five: nineteen of nineteen, then nineteen of twenty one.
8. Nine SNPs is what this organism actually shows.
9. Below it detection falls away. The lower edge again.

## Act 6. What you now have

1. So here is the finished instrument.
2. It returns essentially nothing when nothing is there.
3. It recovers nine tenths of what is really there.
4. And it works only between two measured edges.
5. Both bounds are ranges, not points.
6. The lower edge sits between five eighty eight and seven fifty five.
7. The upper edge, around four thousand seven hundred.
8. That is a ruler. Clip three points it at the data.
