# Clip 1: From one province to a global question

First clip of the TUC briefing series. Long-form, six acts, built as separate
renders and concatenated. All new visuals, no reuse.

**The claim.** The 312 genomes this project sequenced in Nakhon Phanom are not a
provincial dataset that happens to sit in a global panel. They are distributed
through two thirds of the global population structure, which is what makes them
able to answer questions no single province could.

**The quantity that carries it.** The share of the global partition that the
funded isolates reach: 56 of 85 analysis units. The claim is coverage of a
structure, so the quantity is a count against the whole, not a count on its own.

Secondary quantity, act 4: the share of environmental isolates whose analysis unit
also holds a patient isolate, 34 of 42.

**Why motion.** The point is a set of genomes dispersing through a structure that
already exists. A still shows 312 marks somewhere; only motion shows them arriving
from one province and landing across the whole thing. The dispersal is the claim.

## The six acts

One render per act. Each names its entry and exit state. Word budgets are spoken
words; runtime follows from word count at the chosen voice's measured rate, not
from the seconds below, which assume 132 words per minute with a fifth of the act
silent.

**Continuity object: the 312 funded isolates.** They arrive in act 1 and are on
screen, in the same style, for the entire series. Clip 3 act 7 lights them inside
the 85 units; clip 4 act 7 closes the accounting on them. This clip establishes
the mark that the other three pay off.

### Act 1. What this project built (~60 words, ~34 s)
*Entry:* black. *Exit:* 312 marks, one province.

Open on the closeout, not on the science. Three hundred and twenty six isolates
sequenced, 312 confirmed *B. pseudomallei*, 259 from patients and 53 from the
environment. Then the three findings that matter for what follows, in one line
each: the local population is extraordinarily diverse with no dominant clone;
clinical and environmental isolates are drawn from one population; and sharing a
sequence type is not enough to claim a link, so attribution needs core-genome
distance.

That third finding is the hinge of the whole series. It is the previous work
naming the question this work answers.

### Act 2. Two frameworks, reconciled (~70 words, ~40 s)
*Entry:* 312 marks. *Exit:* both panel sizes on screen, related.

Own the discrepancy before anyone finds it. The closeout placed these genomes in a
2,773-genome framework across 35 countries. This work uses a 2,976-genome panel
across 50, of which 2,340 enter analysis because a unit needs a minimum size to be
measurable at all. Same underlying collection, re-curated and expanded for a
different question. Thailand differs by one genome between them, which is the
cleanest evidence of that.

Fifteen seconds spent here removes the only obvious line of attack on the series.

### Act 3. Where the 312 went (~95 words, ~54 s)
*Entry:* both panels. *Exit:* 312 marks distributed across 56 units.

The central act. The partition already exists: 2,340 genomes in 85 analysis units.
Drop the 312 into it. **276 enter the analysed set and land in 56 of the 85
units.** Not a cluster, not a corner. Two thirds of the global structure.

Then the 36 that did not enter, stated plainly and without apology: every one has
a nearest unit, so none is lost. Their lineages are too rare in the global panel
for a measurable unit to form around them. That is a finding about the panel, not
a defect in the isolates, and clip 4 returns to it as the case for what to fund
next.

### Act 4. Patient and environment, at a second scale (~90 words, ~51 s)
*Entry:* 312 across 56 units. *Exit:* the mixed units marked.

The closeout found environmental isolates interspersed among patient isolates
throughout the local population, with no separate environmental lineage. That was
one tree of 312 genomes. Test it again in a structure built independently, from
2,340 genomes across 85 units, that knew nothing about which isolate came from
where.

**Of the 42 environmental isolates in the analysed set, 34 sit in a unit that also
holds a patient isolate. Twenty mixed units, 16 of them in-window.** Same answer,
different instrument, at eight times the scale.

### Act 5. The question this makes askable (~75 words, ~43 s)
*Entry:* the mixed units. *Exit:* the question on screen, unanswered.

Turn to the applied problem. A patient presents with no travel history. The
genome is the only evidence of where the infection was acquired. The closeout
already established that shared sequence type will not answer it and that
core-genome distance is required. So: with core genomes in a global frame, how
far can you actually get? Do not hint at the answer. The remaining clips are the
answer and this act's job is to make the viewer want it.

### Act 6. What the rest of this takes (~32 words, ~18 s)
*Entry:* the question. *Exit:* three titles, handing off to clip 2.

A roadmap in three lines. Before that question can be answered, the measuring
instrument has to be shown to work. Then it has to be pointed at the data. Then
the answer, and its limit.

## What would be a fabrication

- **Implying the 312 were collected to answer the global question.** They were
  sequenced for a provincial study with its own five objectives, all completed.
  This series is a second use of the data, and saying so is the honest and the
  more impressive claim.
- **Drawing the 36 unplaced isolates as a loss, a failure rate or wastage.** They
  are assigned, they have nearest units, and their rarity is information.
- **Presenting the two frameworks as sequential versions** of one panel, or as
  independent datasets. They are one collection curated twice.
- **Carrying a Kawang value without its framework label.**
- **Drawing the 56 units as a majority claim about the species.** 56 of 85 is
  coverage of this panel's structure, not of global *B. pseudomallei* diversity.
- **Any suggestion that the environmental co-occurrence result shows transmission
  from a specific source.** It is a population-level overlap. The closeout's own
  household result, which clip 4 uses, says the source-level claim failed.

## Forbidden framings

- **No country attribution is supported anywhere in this work**, and act 5 must
  pose the question without implying the answer is yes.
- **No dates and no direction of spread.**
- **Never say prior studies were wrong**, and that includes the closeout. Act 1 is
  a recap of work this series depends on, not a critique of it.
- **Do not name cgMLST.** Excluded from this series by instruction, as in the
  earlier deliverable. Describe what the method does, never what it is called.
- **Writing style, hard constraint, anything on screen:** no em dashes, no en
  dashes, no semicolons, no curly quotes, American spelling, median sentence
  length about 19 words.

## Number collision in this clip

**312 and 2,340 and 2,773 and 2,976 are four counts of genomes in four minutes.**
That is the densest stretch of similar quantities in the series and acts 2 and 3
are adjacent. Give each a persistent visual identity in act 2 and reuse it
unchanged for the rest of the series, so later clips never have to re-explain
which number is which.

## Audience

One primary viewer: the TUC laboratory chief who funded the sequencing of the 312
isolates and has seen the closeout deck. She knows the isolates, the province, the
workflow and the provincial findings. She does not need melioidosis background,
the Georgia case, or an explanation of what a phylogeny is, all of which the
closeout already covered. Assume all of it. What is new to her starts at act 2.

## Production constraints

- **1920x1080, 60 fps**, matching the existing clips.
- **Six separate renders, concatenated.** No reuse in this clip.
- **Target about 4 minutes**, roughly 420 spoken words.
- **An animated step for every sentence.**
- **Title card holds 1 to 2 seconds.**
- **The bottom eighth of the frame is reserved** for captions.
- **Narrated, not presenter-led.** Every caveat lives in the visual.
- **The voice is chosen before the runtime is committed**, and it is the same
  voice across all four clips.
- **This clip sets the visual system for the series.** Colors, type, the mark for
  a genome, the mark for a unit and the treatment that distinguishes schematic
  from measured are all established here and reused unchanged in clips 2 to 4.
  Build this one first for that reason, even though clips 3 and 4 were briefed
  first.
- **Two-pass render expected.** Silent preview accepted on content and layout
  only, then narration, then one timing-only lengthen note per act, then the
  delivery re-render.
- **If text is rendered with manim on Linux, guard glyph spacing** by laying out
  at a larger requested size and scaling down.

If the material does not support the claim or the quantity, say so and propose
what it does support. In particular, act 2 is a reconciliation of two numbers and
risks being the dullest 40 seconds in the series. If you can carry it in less
time, or fold it into act 3's dispersal, propose that. It cannot be cut, but it
does not have to be a table.
