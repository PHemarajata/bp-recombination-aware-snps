**At the ν measured in every unit (0.0021–0.0024, A.11ab), Gubbins recovers 91%
of recombination that is really there.** Detection becomes reliable somewhere
between 4 and 9 SNPs per 5 kb tract; the collection sits above that threshold.
**Our r/m values are not systematically deflated by donor similarity.**

at 100% (the top cell is 90%). On 3 replicates and ~20 scorable implants per
cell, 90 vs 100% is about two implants and is sampling noise. The low-ν cells at
20% and 40% are what show the scoring rule is not spuriously generous: a lenient
criterion would have inflated those too.

## THE DEFECT THAT COST THE MOST — read before running anything concurrently

**Gubbins writes intermediates (`<basename>.start`, `.phylip`, `.snp_sites.aln`)
to the CURRENT WORKING DIRECTORY, not to `--prefix`.** Concurrent runs sharing an
alignment basename delete each other's scratch. Symptom:
`FileNotFoundError: '<basename>.start'`, or misleading model-fitting errors.

- Killed ~60–70% of the ν-slice (all 80 used `aln.fa`) **independently of ν**
- Killed 15/15 spike-in v1/v2 (all used `spiked.fa`)
- Never touched the equivalence runs or production (distinct real filenames)
- Tier 2 null escaped by luck (concurrent basenames `rep000.fa`…`rep013.fa`)

**All five runners now `cd` into a per-run directory** — true as of the
2026-08-12 addendum to A.11ai, not before it. When this section was first
written the `cd` was present only in the three *simulation* runners; it was
missing from `treebuilder_equivalence_bp.py` and from the production runner
`reference_sensitivity_bp.py`, both since fixed. Treat this as a trap beside T8.

**The isolating property is the BASENAME, not the path** — scratch is written to
CWD as `<basename>.*`, so distinct paths sharing a filename still collide. Every
"distinct real filenames" escape above was circumstance, not design: across the
184 production arms there are only 30 distinct basenames, and
`aln.full.NC_006350.1.fa`/`NC_006351.1.fa` are shared by 46 arms each. See the
A.11ai addendum for the full re-audit.

**It also cost three wrong conclusions of mine**, all retracted in A.11ai:
"simulated data is unlike real data", "masking depletion", and the ν-slice
negative result itself. The clue was that the failure rate was **flat across a
250-fold ν range** — a failure independent of the variable under study is almost
never about that variable.

of 12 ratios below 1.0 where the two ML builders split 7/12, exactly chance.

**The bias is the tree construction, not the model fitting.** `--tree-builder
rapidnj` is not a pure rapidnj run: Gubbins delegates model fitting to IQ-TREE
(a distance method cannot fit a substitution model). The manifests confirm it —
rapidnj arm records `Model fitter → iqtree`, `Tree constructor → rapidnj`. Row 3
therefore holds the model fitter constant and isolates the constructor, and the
bias survives (p = 0.039). At unit level it moves `s1_L1_19` across the empty band
(2.30 → 2.12) and drags `s2_L1_8` from 5.80 to 4.46, near the 4.28 edge.

**Nothing reported is affected** — every number comes from the RAxML production
arm. But a rapidnj-configured pipeline would report r/m biased low by ~8% median
and up to 46% per replicon. **Action: pin `gubbins_tree_builder = raxml` in the
pipeline config**, and drop `--gubbins_tree_builder rapidnj` from any recorded
invocation. Generalisable lesson now in §2.8.2: verify builder equivalence **per
builder class** — agreement between two ML builders carried no information
about a distance-based one.

The harness is now builder-parameterised: `treebuilder_equivalence_bp.py --run
--builder {iqtree,rapidnj}`, separate output directories, 19 self-tests including
a guard that each comparator is requested explicitly and never silently falls back

### 3e. CALLER INVESTIGATION — ska_map vs snippy (2026-08-12)

**Trigger.** The Nextflow workflow's recombination-aware path uses snippy; the
manual analysis used `ska map`. The pipeline authors warn (measured, this
organism) that split k-mers miss clustered SNPs — precisely the density Gubbins
reads as recombination.

**CONFIRMED: the SNP-level mechanism is real.** Paired arms in
`refsens_cluster37`, SNP recovery by nearest-neighbour spacing
(`caller_spacing_bp.py`, `CALLER_SPACING_RESULT.txt`):

| NN spacing | snippy | ska | ska/snippy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–10 bp | 10,886 | 1,655 | **0.152** |
| 10–31 bp | 11,943 | 8,566 | **0.717** |
| 31–100 bp | 17,899 | 17,606 | 0.984 |
| ≥500 bp | 1,550 | 1,982 | 1.279 |

Depletion stops **exactly at the k=31 split k-mer length** — mechanistically
diagnostic, not a generic quality difference. Independently reproduces the
authors' "~11% within 10 bp".

**REFUTED: mismapping does not explain snippy's clustered excess.** Two
independent tests, both on snippy as its own control (clustered vs isolated):
- nucmer self-alignment: **28 of 2,474** clustered SNPs in repeats even under
  permissive settings (repeat content 2.85%→4.86%).
- RefSeq annotation (K96243 chr1): **21 of 3,144** in mobile elements, **0** in

## A PATTERN WORTH CARRYING FORWARD

Nine errors this session produced **plausible, clean-exiting, wrong output** —
none raised an exception. Every one was caught by comparing a computed quantity
against a known observed value (SNP counts vs the real alignment; tract length vs
our measurement; artefact counts stage by stage), never by the code failing.

the MGE shared-bin alarm, the constant-site union bound); **round-number
thresholds deciding verdicts** (UFBoot 70, the 15% equivalence cutoff at 15.004%,
the 15-point pseudoreplication cutoff); and **rates read off partial
denominators** (four times in one run). The monitors now refuse to display a rate
until its cell is fully resolved.
