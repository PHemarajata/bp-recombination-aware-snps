## If you read only one thing before the synthesis

Four findings from the Gap 4 pass change conclusions elsewhere in the review, not just in Gap 4:

1. **The masked-alignment route is not merely inferior for this organism — it degrades toward an empty alignment.** Didelot & Parkhill 2022, verbatim: extracting non-recombinant sites "works only if relatively few recombination events happened throughout the tree," and in their simulation "there was not a single site that was not affected by recombination on at least one of the branches," so "an alignment containing only the non-recombinant sites would contain no sites." At r/m = 7.2 with 78% of K96243 ever recombined, that is this organism's regime. This is the strongest justification the review has for building on the clonal genealogy, and it upgrades settled fact #2 from an argument to a demonstration.

2. **The sampling frame is inverted against disease burden, and a third of the world's burden is absent entirely.** Sampling intensity spans **35,852-fold** across countries; **Australia is sequenced 1,877× more heavily per predicted case than India**; **29 countries with >100 predicted cases/year have zero genomes, totalling 33% of global predicted burden**, including Indonesia (2nd in the world) and Nigeria (3rd). This is not a bias a model can absorb — see `GAP4 §1.1`.

3. **There is no well-supported between-host molecular clock for this organism.** Chewapreecha's five dated clusters include four that fail their own temporal-signal test once the ranks are read correctly as percentiles; Seng dated 17 isolates of 1,391 and never published their test result. Plan the analysis so a negative dating result is the headline, not a shortfall.

4. **The third dominant BioProject is a case-control study that is 57% environmental and 94% undated** — `PRJEB3409`, 26.3% of the entire collection. It changes what "Thailand" means in this dataset and it is the reason the effective temporal span is 11 years rather than 90.

## Already settled — do not re-research

1. **Sampling is the dominant problem.** Public *B. pseudomallei* assemblies (n = 5,728, NCBI taxid 28450, queried 2026-08-09) are 59.6% Thailand / 10.2% Australia; 81% of Thai genomes come from three BioProjects; only 61.6% have both country and collection year; 50.6% of dated genomes fall in 2015–2019. Raw numbers in `bp_public_genome_audit_2026-08-09.csv`.
   **All three BioProjects are now identified** (2026-08-09; the original audit script never retained `assembly_info.bioproject_accession`, which is why this stayed open — re-run it with that field kept):
   - **PRJEB3409 — 1,506 assemblies, 44.1% of Thai, 26.3% of the whole collection.** ENA title "**Burkholderia_pseudomallei___case_control_study_**", Wellcome Sanger, public 2014-11-26. **56.9% environmental** isolates (856/1,506), 43.2% *Homo sapiens*; **93.6% undated** (1,410/1,506, supplying 91% of all undated Thai genomes; ENA stores the placeholder range "1800/2014", which a naive `date[:4]` parse silently reads as the year 1800).
   - **PRJEB25606 (682) + PRJEB35787 (582)** = Seng et al. 2024, 1,265 clinical isolates, **nine hospitals in northeast Thailand, 2015–2018**, 100% dated, 100% human.
   - Combined: **81.1%**, reproducing the audit figure exactly.
   Two consequences. **~15% of the entire global collection is environmental isolates from one Thai case-control study**, so any comparison against clinical burden is clinical-plus-environmental. And of the **1,860 dated Thai genomes, 1,264 (68%) are Seng's nine hospitals over four years** — that is the concrete mechanism behind the 11-year effective temporal span, and it is textbook confounding of temporal with genetic structure in Murray et al.'s sense.
2. **Build on the recombination-corrected tree, not the masked alignment** (Hedge & Wilson 2014 PMID 25425237; Didelot & Parkhill 2022 PMID 35989600). Gubbins needs full-length pseudogenomes; snp-sites goes *after*, never before.
   **Upgraded from argument to demonstration by the Gap 4 pass**, which retrieved Didelot & Parkhill 2022 in full. Verbatim: the masked-alignment method "works only if relatively few recombination events happened throughout the tree"; in their simulation "there was not a single site that was not affected by recombination on at least one of the branches," so "an alignment containing only the non-recombinant sites would contain no sites, and could not be used as a starting point for further analysis. On the other hand, the inferred clonal genealogy … can be used." Their Figure 1 is this exact pipeline. Also from that paper: variant-site alignments "cannot be used for recombination-aware phylogenetics since the genomic distance between variant sites becomes an important factor." Detail in `GAP4 §5.0`.
3. **Cluster-then-Gubbins is the field standard** (Chewapreecha 2017 PMID 28112723: hierBAPS → per-lineage Gubbins → per-cluster and per-chromosome BEAST → stochastic character mapping on subsampled trees). ~~The graft onto an unmasked backbone has no published precedent.~~ **Corrected 2026-08-09: PopPIPE (PMID 40294103) does exactly this graft** — but rescales subtree branch lengths onto one global distance scale first, and its authors label the product a visualization that "will not maximize the phylogenetic likelihood." Adopt the rescaling; keep the topology-only caveat.
   **Qualified again by the Gap 3 pass (2026-08-09), in two ways.** PopPIPE's Gubbins appears to sit on the `transmission` branch rather than the path feeding `graft_tree`, so its grafted tree may be assembled from subtrees that were never recombination-corrected — inferred from the Snakefile, not stated by the authors, and worth one run to settle. And **PopPIPE is no longer the only pipeline doing cluster-then-per-cluster-recombination**: ARETE does it at >10,000 genomes, BigBacter in routine surveillance, both stopping before the graft. Meanwhile the graft itself is proven at SARS-CoV-2 scale (COG-UK grapevine / virus-evolution phylopipe via `clusterfunk graft`) with recombination assumed away. So the honest claim is narrower and stronger than "no precedent": **the unsolved piece is the merge, under recombination.** Also note the principled version of that merge exists and is uncited — NJMerge, TreeMerge, and GTM, the last of which provably minimises topological distance to the guide tree.
4. **Clusters are biologically real in this organism** — clade-specific restriction-modification systems restrict inter-clade gene flow (Nandi 2015 PMID 25236617).
5. **Verticall — benchmarks read in full 2026-08-09; the recommendation now splits in two.** (Odih, Wick & Holt, bioRxiv 2026-04-24, DOI 10.64898/2026.04.21.719734; paper describes **v0.4.2**.) Full detail in §6 of `GAP1_reference_free_kmer_methods.md`.
   - **Verticall *distance* — use it, per cluster.** Recovered temporal signal in **63/83 (75.9%)** real *K. pneumoniae* clonal sublineages of 19–838 genomes, against Gubbins' **42/83 (50.6%)**. On *S. pneumoniae* PMEN1 it matched Gubbins on root-to-tip R² (0.51 each) and dated the root to 1970 against Gubbins' 1974 and the published 1972. Directly relevant to Gap 4's weak-clock problem. **But it is O(n²) and timed out at 4,857 genomes** — fine per cluster, useless species-wide.
   - **Verticall *alignment* — never for dating.** It under-filters badly (median 2 recombination blocks per genome vs Gubbins' 17 on PMEN1), recovered no more temporal signal than no filtering at all (R² 0.17 vs 0.15 unfiltered), and dated PMEN1's root to **1701 [1186–1915] instead of ~1972**.
   - **Not a backbone solution.** The distance workflow won't reach 3,000+ genomes; the alignment workflow's tree-building timed out on the one genus-wide dataset tested. The hope that Verticall might rescue the between-cluster level is not supported.
   - Worth stealing from their methods: QC at N50 ≥ 15 kb and ≤1,000 contigs; **Treemmer v0.3** downsampling above 200 genomes per lineage, keeping ≥200 plus at least one per unique metadata combination (a citable subsampling protocol for Gap 4); and **a separate reference genome per sublineage**, applied across 83 lineages — independent validation of the per-cluster-reference design.

## ~~Gap 1 — Reference-free and k-mer methods~~ — CLOSED 2026-08-09

**Resolved. See `GAP1_reference_free_kmer_methods.md`** (with `ska_feasibility_bp.py` for the divergence and memory arithmetic). Summary of what changed:

- **SKA2 and `ska lo` are within-cluster tools, and *B. pseudomallei* is inside their range at cluster level.** Species-wide π ≈ 0.0067 (derived), corroborated by Chewapreecha's per-isolate maximum of 0.596% divergence from K96243 — just past SKA2's strain boundary, ~8× below the collapse regime. `ska map` feeds Gubbins via the officially supported `generate_ska_alignment.py`, ~190× faster than Snippy, with correct multi-replicon coordinates. `ska align` must never reach Gubbins.
- **PopPIPE: adopt the design, not the software.** It is Snakemake against your Nextflow, there is no *Burkholderia* PopPUNK database, and it has only been shown at 616 genomes of a 2.1 Mb organism. But **it grafts subtrees onto a backbone exactly as you do** — which retires the "no published precedent" claim in §4 of the review — and it rescales branch lengths onto one global scale first, which is the piece your implementation is missing.
- **Core-genome fraction: the review's "86% of K96243" is a 2008 microarray figure.** Sequencing-era equivalents are ~76%, and the fraction erodes with sample size. Chewapreecha 2017 never reports a callable fraction at all.
- **Reference choice is the largest controllable error source, and *B. pseudomallei* sits in the worst part of the range.** Across 209 pipelines and ten species, distance-to-reference predicts recall at ρ = −0.94, worst for recombinogenic organisms (Bush 2020, PMID 32025702). A 0.82% divergent within-species reference inflated false positives ~100× in *Listeria* (PMID 25144537); *B. pseudomallei* spans 0.73–5.61% from K96243. **Recombination inference is itself reference-dependent** (PMID 33503026) — which puts the Gubbins step downstream of an unvalidated choice. Only one small *B. pseudomallei* study has ever varied the reference (Webb 2022, PMID 35080450: distant reference under-called by ~20% at outbreak scale). Adopt "≥2 references, report concordance" as standing practice; full experiment in §11 of the new document.
- **Pangenome tooling is complementary, not substitutive** — Gubbins forbids core-gene concatenations. **Graph references are measured to be worse at core SNPs specifically:** Pandora (PMID 34521456), the tool purpose-built for bacterial pangenome graphs, reports **~11% loss of recall at core SNPs** and a 20–30× higher error rate than snippy, while winning on rare/accessory variants. pggb adds practical obstacles: 500 complete *E. coli* is the published bacterial ceiling, drafts have never been benchmarked, `-x auto` sparsification assumes panmixia this organism violates. No graph work exists anywhere in *Burkholderia*. PulseNet's answer to reference sensitivity was wgMLST, not graphs.

**Spillover: Gap 5 is now fully closed** — see its section below (Nandi 2015: r/m = 7.2 genome-wide, 78% of K96243 recombined, both verified). **Gap 2 gains two tools** (iterative-PopPUNK PMID 37253539, fastbaps PMID 31076776) plus a calibration point explaining why sketch clustering splits finer than BAPS. **Gap 3 gains** Spring-Pearson's 96%/4% recombination model and Nandi's list of 1,630 high-recombination genes as the basis for a masked-region BED, plus a published precedent for skipping ascertainment correction entirely.

**Wu et al. 2026 obtained and read 2026-08-09** (§8 of the new document). Core alignment **3,805,619 bp = 52.5% of K96243**; ten clusters **imposed** via `fcluster(t=10)`, dissolving the Gap 2 puzzle; constant sites retained instead of `+ASC`, giving Gap 3 a precedent; reference genome for SNP calling never stated. The "5.61% divergence" figure reverse-engineers to a consistent ~772 kb denominator, so it is not genome-wide SNP divergence and does not affect the SKA assessment.

**Spring-Pearson's core counts resolved 2026-08-09**: 3,278 strict / 4,736 extended are the **observed** counts across 37 genomes; 2,798 ± 59 and 4,568 ± 16 are **asymptotic estimates** from 100 permutations of sequencing order. Both correct, different questions — never mix them.

~~Still open from this gap: the length of Chewapreecha's core alignment, which the paper never reports, leaving the ~772 kb divergence denominator unidentified.~~ **CLOSED by the Gap 4 pass (2026-08-09).** The full 27-page Supplementary Information was retrieved and text-searched: **no core-genome alignment length appears anywhere**, total or per chromosome, in main text or SI. This is a definitive non-report. The ~772 kb denominator is arithmetically recoverable (5,650 ÷ 0.0073 ≈ 774 kb; 43,221 ÷ 0.0561 ≈ 770 kb) but **reconciles with nothing** — it is 10.7% of K96243's 7,247,547 bp, implausibly small for a core genome, while the one-decimal-shifted reading implies ~7.72 Mb, which exceeds the reference. The quantity "genetic divergence compared with the K96243 core genome" is never defined. **Drop the 772 kb figure rather than propagating it.**

## ~~Gap 2 — Clustering algorithms~~ — CLOSED 2026-08-09

**Resolved. See `GAP2_clustering_algorithms.md`** (with `cluster_diagnostics_bp.py` for the size-distribution diagnostic and the Gubbins contrast arithmetic). Summary of what changed:

- **The framing was backwards.** Seng et al.'s PopPUNK run on 1,391 *B. pseudomallei* genomes produced **101 lineages**. Your 61–76 over roughly twice as many genomes is *coarser* than the only in-species precedent, not finer. Stop treating the cluster count as the problem.
- **fastbaps, in phylogeny-conditioned mode.** rhierbaps **did not finish in a week** on 3,156 genomes × 392 kb — almost exactly your scale — while fastbaps took 795 s. `best_baps_partition()` on a tree is the only BAPS-family mode that cannot emit a polyphyletic cluster, runs linear-time, and is what PopPIPE uses.
- **Nobody has tested whether BAPS-family or PopPUNK clusters track clonal descent under high r/m.** The two results that look reassuring both score against deme membership or simulated tree nodes. Both models are products of independent per-site likelihoods with no recombination process. This is a real hole and is worth stating in print.
- **Chewapreecha's stopping rule cannot be implemented as written — Gubbins publishes no numeric divergence ceiling.** Confirmed absent from the paper, manual, docs site and manpage. Nobody implements the rule: PopPIPE hard-codes two fastbaps levels and `min_cluster_size: 6`; iterative-PopPUNK gives a 30-point sweep across 1–99% of maximum average core distance but no criterion; rPinecone is a *post*-Gubbins tool for low-variant populations.
- **But it is derivable, and there is an in-organism calibration point.** Gubbins detects a local excess over background, so the binding quantity is a contrast ratio: species-wide it collapses to ~0.7×, at Seng's within-lineage diversity it is 35–54×. Seng ran PopPUNK → per-lineage Gubbins v3.1.3 on lineages of **351, 517 and 549 mean pairwise core SNPs**. Adopt a cap of ~1,000 and cite that. Note that the obvious mechanism-matched proxy — Gubbins' window-sizing rule — turns out **not** to bind at any realistic *B. pseudomallei* branch length.
- **Fixed SNP thresholds are indefensible here, with a number from your own organism.** Sarovich 2017: two *B. pseudomallei* STs at 1,328 raw SNPs, 36 recombination blocks totalling ~339 kb, **73 SNPs (~5%) left after masking** — an ~18× inflation. Webb 2022 states in terms that no *B. pseudomallei* threshold has ever been established. Hennart 2022 shows recombinant genomes bridging and fusing groups under single linkage.
- **Mash is the wrong primitive and sketch size does not save it.** At >99.9% ANI, Mash's correlation with true ANI is ~0.01 even at a 100,000-hash sketch. Check what sketch size the current pipeline used; at the default s=1,000 the distance error at within-species range is ~14% relative.
- **A full validation stack is now citable**: PopPUNK network score against a documented ≥0.8 bar (Seng's published fit scores 0.8961, so it passes); directional adjusted Wallace against the Lichtenegger cgMLST scheme; treespace/Kendall-Colijn at λ=0 using within-scheme bootstrap dispersion as the null; core-versus-accessory refit congruence; per-cluster bootstrap Jaccard with Hennig's thresholds; and the cluster-size distribution check, which is implemented and ready to run.
- **Embeddings visualise, never define.** HDBSCAN on a mandrake embedding of 20,047 pneumococcal genomes recovered PopPUNK's GPSCs at **AMI 0.085**, and in the same authors' bacterial simulations "the UMAP embedding rarely reflected the underlying simulated population structure."

**Spillover into Gap 3**: Gubbins current release is **v3.4.3, tagged 2025-08-27**, and the v3.4.2/v3.4.3 release notes are specifically *"Fixes to the invariant site calculations"* — which is the version-dependent behaviour Gap 3 flags as a correctness risk, now pinned to a version and a date. Also: `--filter-percentage` is a **missing-data** filter (default 25% gaps), *not* a divergence filter, so a hyper-divergent isolate that aligns cleanly passes straight through. And **nf-core/bactmap has no clustering step at all** — Gubbins is an optional flag applied to the whole alignment — so it is not adoptable as-is for a cluster-then-Gubbins design.

**Spillover into Gap 4**: re-clustering a geographically and BioProject-balanced subsample and comparing partitions is a direct test of whether the clusters are biology or sequencing history; Meirmans 2018 (*Heredity* 122(3):276–287) is the transferable citation, having shown that unbalanced sampling drove 10 of 12 species to a spurious K=2 that balanced subsampling dissolved.

**Corrections to this file made by that pass**: `treespace v1.14.3` below should read **v. 1.1.4.3** as printed in Seng's Methods; rPinecone is **Wailan et al. 2019**, not 2020, and is post-Gubbins rather than a candidate partitioner; and Lichtenegger 2021 does not *refuse* a threshold, it simply never proposes one.

Still open from this gap: whether any clustering method's output tracks clonal descent at *B. pseudomallei* recombination levels — the same simulation that would answer the main review's SimBac recommendation would answer this too.

### Original notes, retained as context

**The Wu-et-al. discrepancy is already dissolved.** Their full text was obtained 2026-08-09. Their ten clusters were **imposed, not inferred** — `fcluster(criterion="maxclust", t=10)` on a UPGMA dendrogram of pairwise SNP distances, i.e. "give me exactly ten." Cluster sizes run 285–583, a max/min ratio of 2.0×, which is the signature of a fixed-*k* cut rather than population structure. **Do not treat 10 as a target cluster count or as evidence your 61–76 is wrong.** The real comparators are Chewapreecha's 19 hierBAPS groups and whatever fastbaps / iterative-PopPUNK give on your data. Details in §8 of `GAP1_reference_free_kmer_methods.md`.

**Run this diagnostic before anything else.** Chewapreecha's per-isolate cluster assignments are now tabulated in `chewapreecha_hierbaps_clusters_2026-08-09.csv` (from their Supplementary Data 1). Their 19 hierBAPS groups range from **4 to 137 isolates, median 17, a max/min ratio of 34.2×**, with a further 7.2% of isolates dumped in an unassigned "bin". Wu's imposed ten-way cut has a ratio of 2.0× and bins nothing.

So: **compute the size distribution of your own 61–76 Mash clusters.** Roughly even sizes mean the clustering is imposing structure; a strongly skewed distribution with a long tail and some hard-to-place isolates means it is finding it. That single check is more diagnostic than comparing your cluster count against 19 or 10.

**Three more facts from that table, all bearing on Gap 2:**
- **Clusters are essentially geographic.** 19 of 20 labels are ≥90% one region. Group 1 is 100% Australasia; every other named group is 100% Asia except Group 19 (Africa/Americas). **No Asia/Australasia mixing at cluster level** — Wallace's Line is in the clustering itself.
- **Group 1 is the problem child**: 137 isolates (29% of the collection), 98 distinct STs, 100% Australasian, explicitly "could not be further sub-clustered", and not dateable. Australia is 586 genomes in your collection — expect the same at four times the size, in the cluster that carries the phylogeographic signal.
- **MLST assigns clusters tolerably but resolves topology poorly.** Only 14 of 211 STs (6.6%) span more than one cluster, mostly the "STunknown" placeholder. But **ST105 spans Group 1 (Australasia) and Group 15 (Asia)** — independently reproducing De Smet's intercontinental homoplasy case — and ST51 spans three clusters. Do not use ST as a grouping variable.

**Chewapreecha's stopping rule, now quoted verbatim from the Methods (read 2026-08-09):**

> "A tree-independent hierarchical Bayesian clustering with hierBAPS was employed to determine the population structure generated from the core genome mapping alignment. This method allows the population to be sub-divided into groups with closely related genetic backgrounds and allows the recombination detection tool (Gubbins) to operate within its best performing range. Except for the Australasian cluster (Group 1), which contained the highest amount of diversity for each isolate and could not be further sub-clustered, we continued the hierarchical clustering **until the diversity observed in secondary or tertiary clusters fell within the limit of recombination detection.** This resulted in 19 groups."

Two caveats the review does not currently record. **Group 1, the Australasian cluster, could not be sub-clustered at all** — it was too diverse, so the rule failed on precisely the population carrying the phylogeographic signal. And **Group 15 plus a 35-isolate "bin" cluster were not monophyletic**; only Groups 1–14 and 16–19 formed monophyletic groups. So 19 is not 19 clean lineages.

**PopPUNK has already been fitted to this organism** (Seng et al. 2024, PMID 38972886): v2.6.0 on **1,391 genomes**, `--min-k 15 --max-kmer 31 --max-a-dist 0.53 --K 4 --k-step 2`, giving density 0.028, transitivity 0.992, network score 0.8961. Start from those parameters rather than fitting blind. They also used **rhierbaps v1.1.4** to split lineages into sub-lineages, and compared core-SNP / cgMLST / MLST / PopPUNK topologies with **treespace v1.14.3** — finding core-SNP and cgMLST trees congruent while MLST trees dispersed widely.

Two further tools identified in Gap 1, each supplying the principled criterion the current Mash clustering lacks — do not re-research these:
- **iterative-PopPUNK** (Zhao et al., *Genome Res.* 2023;33(6):988–998, PMID 37253539) — sweeps cluster assignments across a range of sequence identities so you can *choose* a resolution rather than guess one.
- **fastbaps** (Tonkin-Hill et al., *Nucleic Acids Res.* 2019;47(11):5539–5549, PMID 31076776) — partitions an existing hierarchy to maximise DPM marginal likelihood; a model-based stopping rule. PopPIPE runs it constrained to partition the phylogeny, which eliminates polyphyletic clusters by construction.

Calibration point: PopPUNK split 616 pneumococcal genomes into 62 strains where BAPS gave 16. **Sketch-based clustering splitting several-fold finer than BAPS is expected behaviour**, so 61–76 is not prima facie wrong.

The current pipeline uses Mash distances with no principled cluster-size criterion, yielding 61–76 clusters.

## ~~Gap 3 — Pipelines and scale~~ — CLOSED 2026-08-09

**Resolved. See `GAP3_pipelines_and_scale.md`** (with `pipeline_checks_bp.py` for four runnable checks: the `-fconst` accounting, the replicon split plus per-replicon callable fraction, junction-artefact detection in Gubbins GFF output, and the distinct-site-pattern cost measurement). Summary of what changed:

- **Take nf-core modules, not the pipeline — and bactmap's own docs are the best citation for your architecture.** On `--remove_recombination` they say it *"should only be run on sets of samples that are closely related and not for example on a set of samples that have diversity spanning that of the entire species."* Meanwhile bactmap 2.0.0's dev branch **deletes Gubbins and all four tree builders**. Every step you need exists as a maintained module (`gubbins`, `iqtree`, `snpsites`, `snpdists`, `parsnp`, `mash/*`, `ska/*`); `poppunk` does not.
- **The novelty claim narrows, and improves.** ARETE does cluster → per-cluster Gubbins/Verticall at **>10,000 genomes**; BigBacter does it in routine public-health surveillance. **Both stop deliberately before grafting.** PopPIPE is still the only bacterial pipeline that grafts — but its Gubbins appears to sit on the `transmission` branch, *not* the path feeding `graft_tree`, which **qualifies settled fact #3 above**. The graft is proven at scale only in SARS-CoV-2 (COG-UK grapevine / phylopipe, `clusterfunk graft`), where recombination is assumed away. **The unsolved piece is specifically the merge, under recombination.**
- **Use `-fconst`, never `+ASC`, and the reason is compositional.** They are Leaché's reconstituted-DNA and conditional-likelihood corrections. K96243 is **68.06% GC** (A 15.9 / C 34.1 / G 33.9 / T 16.0); `-fconst` with true counts reproduces full-alignment base frequencies and tree length exactly, while `+ASC` and flat counts both collapse to ≈25/25/25/25.
- **`+ASC` does not silently inflate branch lengths — it hard-errors.** The hazard is the remedy: the trigger is `frac_invariant_sites`, so Gubbins-masked columns with one base plus gaps trip it, and rerunning on `.varsites.phy` **silently deletes them**.
- **Gubbins v3.4.2 silently flipped its internal invariant-site correction to OFF and shipped a broken model-selection chain.** Pin **≥3.4.3** and pass `--invariant-site-correction` explicitly. The v3.4.2 release note is *"Make invariant site correction optional"*, not what this file previously recorded.
- **Split by replicon before Gubbins.** `snippy-core` concatenates with no separator; Gubbins scans with a 0.1–10 kb sliding window across the junction; `snp-sites` **hardcodes `CHROM` to `"1"`**. Gubbins has no `--mask`, no BED input, no per-contig option, and its manual never mentions replicons — but **Theiagen's docs state the incompatibility flatly**: *"If reference genomes have multiple contigs, they are incompatible with Gubbins."*
- **Nobody has compared concatenated versus per-replicon phylogeny, for any organism** — and this lab has done it both ways without comment (Chewapreecha 2017 split; Seng 2024 concatenated). Chewapreecha's American-isolate TMRCA is **1806 from chr I and 1759 from chr II**. The strongest argument for splitting is Dillon 2015: chr II has the *lowest* mutation rate of the three replicons yet the *highest* observed evolutionary rate, so the excess is selection and recombination, not mutation.
- **No published K96243 mask BED — but the coordinates exist.** Holden 2004: **16 GIs = 6.1% of the genome**. Tuanyok 2008: **71 GIs across five strains** including K96243. Summary of what changed:

- **The strongest argument in this gap needs no model, and nobody has made it in this organism.** Limmathurotsakul 2016 (PMID 26877885). Regionally: **South Asia carries 44.2% of predicted global cases and holds 2.9% of genomes; East Asia & Pacific carries 39.4% and holds 93.5%.** At country level (Supplementary Table 1, in the **publisher** supplement only — the NIH author-manuscript version has figures but no tables): sampling intensity spans **35,852-fold**; **Australia is sequenced 1,877× more heavily per predicted case than India**; India has the world's largest national burden at **52,506 cases/yr (22,335–124,652)** and 110 genomes; the Philippines has 9,116 cases/yr and **one** genome. **Twenty-nine countries with >100 predicted cases/yr have zero genomes, together 54,076 cases/yr = 33% of global predicted burden** — including **Indonesia (20,038/yr, 2nd in the world)** and **Nigeria (13,481/yr, 3rd)**. Note also that **Cambodia now has zero genomes** despite being one of the six countries in Chewapreecha's balanced design. Also verbatim: melioidosis "is likely endemic in a further 34 countries which have never reported the disease." Full tables in `GAP4 §1` and `§1.1`; arithmetic in `phylogeography_diagnostics_bp.py §F` and `§F2`.
- **DTA is indefensible here for a structural reason, not a matter of degree.** De Maio 2015: "the relative sampling intensities of the demes are **treated as data, informative about the migration parameters, even before any sequence data is analysed**." The test is not "how skewed?" but "is the count vector an estimate of the population-size vector?" — and §1 answers no. Their no-data experiment returned a posterior migration log-ratio of **1.7** from sampling counts alone.
- **But the structured coalescent is NOT the alternative. This reverses the obvious move.** Layan 2023 (PMID 36860641), the only graded head-to-head: "**BASTA and MASCOT reconstructions were also biased when employing unbiased samples**"; they "compensate for location under-representation by **estimating high backward-in-time migration rates to the under-represented location**" — so ~20 singleton countries would become the inferred migration hubs. And "**it currently seems unlikely that such analyses are possible at all with BASTA and MASCOT**" above 15 locations. Scaling is O(N·S³)/O(N²·S²). The largest geographic structured-coalescent run in *any* bacterium is 260 *V. cholerae* genomes / 11 demes.
- **MASCOT-GLM, the one fix that worked under high bias, is unavailable** — it needs per-country case counts. Belman 2024 (PMID 38507601) hit the identical wall for pneumococcus and abandoned the family: "for an endemic, often asymptomatic pathogen this remains difficult."
- **The answer is design-based — but balanced subsampling is a TRADE, not a free correction, and at this bias level the trade may be bad.** Layan's endorsement is regime-qualified ("at intermediate sampling bias"). The SAASI authors benchmarked `ace` and `simmap` directly and found that once you count the internal nodes downsampling deletes, "**the accuracy drops dramatically, and is substantially lower than reconstructions on the full tree**." Their breakpoint is between **4× and 10×** imbalance — at 10×, `ace` "incorrectly infers approximately half of the internal node states" and "**misidentifies the root state as** [the over-sampled state]." Thailand:Australia is ≈5.8× and the within-Thailand study-level imbalance is far worse, so this collection is at or past that line. The price is also measured: at n=15 Chewapreecha's rule keeps **225 of 5,515 genomes (4.1%)** and discards 22 countries; **n=100 is the better operating point** (8 countries, 800 genomes).
- **Directional warning that bears on the Australian-origin claim.** SAASI: "if state [i] is far less sampled … ace **overestimates the transition rates from other states to state [i]**." Australia is the under-sampled state, so rates *into* Australia are inflated and rates *out of* Australia deflated. "Lineages moved from Southeast Asia into Australia" is exactly the conclusion this bias manufactures.
- **Two cheap corrections beat subsampling and are implementable today.** (i) A **tip-state-swap permutation null** — permute country labels across tips holding the tree fixed, re-run `make.simmap`, compare observed transition counts and root state against the null; then repeat permuting **BioProject** labels. This is the mechanism behind Gámbaro 2025's adjusted Bayes factor and it is the only handle anyone has on the study-of-origin confounder. (ii) A **sampling-corrected root prior** — SAASI's own simmap baseline used `pi = (n_i/s_i)/Σ(n_j/s_j)`, not the flat default, and the burden estimates supply s_i. One line in phytools, aimed at the exact failure mode carrying the origin claim.
- **Do NOT use Treemmer for the phylogeographic subsample.** Its authors state it "should not be considered as a random unbiased sample: the number of leaves … depends on the genetic diversity of the different clades and **not on the abundance** of different clades in nature." A discrete-trait ASR is frequency-driven, so Treemmer swaps one wrong sampling model for another. Fine for tractability and for the Verticall use case; not a phylogeographic fix.
- **Study of origin is the dominant unaddressed confounder and the field has no machinery for it.** Searched and not found: any bacterial-genomics paper treating BioProject as a random effect or stratification variable. Context: Blackwell 2021 (PMID 34752446) found **50% of 661,405 ENA genomes come from 50 of 23,316 projects**. Also not found: inverse-probability tip weighting, an effective number of independent isolates, a discrete-trait phylogenetic ESS, or any justification in print for one-isolate-per-patient deduplication.
- **Reporting: STROME-ID items 6.1, 9.1, 12.1, 12.2 and especially 13.2** (sampling fraction plus cluster-size distribution for cluster-based studies) are the only formal requirements that bite. Compliance across 114 TB genomic-epidemiology papers averaged **50%** and did not improve after publication (Cheng 2021, PMID 33842904) — reporting them would put the paper in a small minority.
- **Dateability cannot be triaged from cluster metadata.** Every predictor scored against Chewapreecha's own dated/not-dated outcome sits near AUC 0.5. A span-based filter was built, validated and **failed**; the failure is reported rather than the filter.
- **The date-randomisation test is anticonservative under exactly this data structure** (Murray 2016, PMID 27110344: >1/3 of confounded replicates passed while giving a tMRCA of 51 years against a true 10,000). Their danger zone is "fewer than **7** substitutions per genome" across the window; this collection's **effective** span of 11.05 independent-year-equivalents at the Pearson rate gives **7.1**. Use **BETS** (PMID 32895707) — the only test that can positively support *absence* of signal.
- **Tree-free methods scale further than assumed but cannot substitute.** fineSTRUCTURE has run at 4,067 *H. pylori* and 1,128 *E. faecium* genomes; van Hal 2022 (PMID 35146465) is an adoptable template. But painting cannot date, ADMIXTURE is fatally exposed to unbalanced sampling (Lawson 2018, PMID 30108219), and **fastGEAR resolves ancestral direction by "always mark the lineage with fewer strains as the recipient"** — which on this collection mechanically declares Australia the recipient.
- **The pipeline paper was retrieved and it settles the architecture question.** Didelot & Parkhill 2022 (*Phil Trans R Soc B* 377:20210246) is the step-by-step Gubbins→BactDating guidance paper, and its Figure 1 is exactly this pipeline. The decisive passage, verbatim: extracting non-recombinant sites into a masked alignment "**works only if relatively few recombination events happened throughout the tree**," and in their simulation "**there was not a single site that was not affected by recombination on at least one of the branches**," so "an alignment containing only the non-recombinant sites would contain no sites, and could not be used as a starting point for further analysis. On the other hand, the inferred clonal genealogy … can be used." At r/m = 7.2 with 78% of K96243 ever recombined, that is this organism's regime — the strongest available justification for building on the clonal genealogy. Also from that paper: **BactDating handles missing dates natively** ("treating the missing dates as additional parameters that are inferred simultaneously"), which is the route for the 38.2% undated rather than dropping tips; the date-randomisation floor is "**at least 20 randomized datasets**"; and rooting is a dating decision (turn off root estimation if outgroups already fixed it). Detail in `GAP4 §5.0`.
- **A counterpoint worth carrying so the review does not overreach.** Viberg et al. 2017 (verified) state that this organism "demonstrates a very strong phylogeographic signal that allows accurate identification of strain origin **on a continental level**," and they used it to correctly assign one CF patient's infection to Southeast Asia against known travel history. So: continental-level origin is well supported and independently validated; dated, directional, between-country migration inference is not. Say both.
- **Stochastic character mapping survives a dating failure, but one of its two outputs does not.** `make.simmap` needs only a `phylo` object — no dated tree — so it runs on a substitution-scaled tree. But `maps`/`mapped.edge` record "times spent in each state" *in the tree's branch-length units*, so **transition counts and directions survive an undated tree while occupancy times do not**. Also: phytools is now **2.5-2** against Chewapreecha's **0.5-10**; the default model is `SYM`, so their `ARD` was a deliberate override; and the manual records a **root-node sampling error affecting user-supplied `pi` priors in "phytools 1.0-1 and probably prior recent versions."** The root state *is* the Australian-origin claim and the paper never reports `pi` — worth one re-run.
- **Highest-value concrete recommendation:** build **geographically balanced environmental reference panels** and run a Campylobacter-style probabilistic source attribution (Wilson 2008, PMID 18818764). That is a sampling investment, not a modelling one.

**Two further absences worth claiming in print:** no bacterial precedent exists for **balanced-subsample re-clustering with a partition-agreement statistic** (PopPUNK supplies the ARI machinery, Meirmans 2018 the motivation), and no phytools facility or published guidance exists for **pooling stochastic maps across trees with different tip sets** — which is exactly what Chewapreecha-style resampling produces, so only transition counts and root state are poolable, and only as a distribution over replicates rather than a posterior.
