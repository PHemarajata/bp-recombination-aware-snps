## Clip 2 was never silent. The wrong file was easy to click

Measured segment by segment, every part of clip 2 carries audio between -17.1 and
-20.7 LUFS. Only its 5 second title card is silent, and that is by design.

**Each working folder held a silent `TUC_CLIPn.mp4` at its top level**, with the
narrated cut buried in `final/`. The silent one is the obvious thing to open. This
is the same shape as the APHL package's stale `legacy_mp4/`, which this project has
already been caught by once.

Fixed so it cannot happen again:

- the four narrated films are copied into one folder, named for what they are
- every silent picture is renamed **`PICTURE_ONLY_NO_AUDIO_clipN.mp4`**, so the
  filename says so before anyone clicks it
- clip 1's superseded 291 s cut, which still sat in `final/` beside the current
  279 s one, is renamed `final_SUPERSEDED_291s/`

## Clip 2 does open with 6.2 s of silence, and that may have contributed

It is the only clip with a separate 5 second title card, so it is the only one
that opens quiet. Clips 1, 3 and 4 speak within 1.8 s.

**An attempt to narrate the title card was withdrawn.** A line short enough to fit
5 seconds is refused by `build_narration.py`, which computes silence from the
nominal rate rather than the measured one: nine words reads as 40% silent at 179
wpm, while the engine actually read that line at **129 wpm**, which is 16%. The
gate and the engine disagree, and on a card this short the gate cannot be
satisfied by anything that also fits.

Options, none of them taken without a decision: shorten the title card to about
2.5 s, move act 1's opening line earlier, or leave it.

## A trap worth recording

**`build_narration.py` leaves the previous CSV in place when it refuses.** The
refusal is printed, but the next step reads the stale CSV and regenerates the
*old* text. That is how a line came back byte-identical after being rewritten, and
it is only visible if you compare the CSV against the spec.
