## Clip 2 really was silent, and my first diagnosis was wrong

**Corrected.** I first measured clip 2 with `ebur128` and per-segment loudness,
saw normal levels throughout, and concluded the wrong file had been opened. That
was wrong: `final/TUC_CLIP2_NARRATED.mp4` did play silent.

**The cause was a channel-layout mismatch.** The title card is first in the
concat list and I had generated its silence as **stereo**; every other part comes
out of `assemble_voice.py` as **mono**. Concatenating with `-c copy` takes the
first stream's parameters, so the container declared stereo while carrying mono
frames for the other 299 seconds. ffmpeg decodes that anyway, which is exactly why
my check passed it, and a normal player follows the container and renders silence.

**Why the check could not see it.** `ebur128` and per-segment loudness both run
through ffmpeg's tolerant decoder. A test that decodes with the same library that
wrote the file cannot detect a container that lies about its own contents. What
catches it is comparing the stream parameters of every part before concatenating.

Fixed: the title card is regenerated as mono and clip 2 re-concatenated.
**Verified by full decode**: 304.5 s of audio, 31 windows of 10 s, none silent,
levels -20.4 to -25.6 dB.

**The other three were never affected.** Their parts are uniformly mono because
none of them needed a synthetic silent segment. All four now full-decode with
continuous audio and no silent window.

## The folder trap was real too, and is also fixed

Each working folder held a silent `TUC_CLIPn.mp4` at its top level with the
narrated cut buried in `final/`. That is still worth fixing even though it was not
the cause here:

- the four narrated films are copied into one folder, named for what they are
- every silent picture is renamed **`PICTURE_ONLY_NO_AUDIO_clipN.mp4`**
- clip 1's superseded 291 s cut is renamed `final_SUPERSEDED_291s/`

## Clip 2 does open with 6.2 s of silence, and that may have contributed

It is the only clip with a separate 5 second title card, so it is the only one
that opens quiet. Clips 1, 3 and 4 speak within 1.8 s.

**An attempt to narrate the title card was withdrawn.** A line short enough to fit
5 seconds is refused by `build_narration.py`, which computes silence from the
nominal rate rather than the measured one: nine words reads as 40% silent at 179
wpm, while the engine actually read that line at **129 wpm**, which is 16%. The
gate and the engine disagree, and on a card this short the gate cannot be
satisfied by anything that also fits.

Options, none of them taken without a decision: shorten the title card to about
2.5 s, move act 1's opening line earlier, or leave it.

## A trap worth recording

**`build_narration.py` leaves the previous CSV in place when it refuses.** The
refusal is printed, but the next step reads the stale CSV and regenerates the
*old* text. That is how a line came back byte-identical after being rewritten, and
it is only visible if you compare the CSV against the spec.
