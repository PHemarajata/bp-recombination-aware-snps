# The APHL film on eleven_v3, loudness normalized

Rebuilt 2026-09-17 after review: "still a little inconsistent, some parts more
enunciated and louder than others."
Deliverables: `~/.agi/renders/4f9166a4-.../film_v3/`. Runtime 213.121354 s.

## The loudness complaint was measurable, and is fixed

| | quietest | loudest | spread | stdev |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| previous build, turbo | -29.4 | -21.2 | **8.2 LU** | 2.19 |
| raw v3, before normalizing | -22.2 | -18.7 | 3.4 LU | 0.84 |
| **v3, normalized to -20 LUFS** | **-21.6** | **-20.3** | **1.3 LU** | **0.30** |

Three LU is a clearly audible step and six reads as roughly twice as loud, so an
8.2 LU spread across 31 lines is exactly what "some parts louder than others"
sounds like. Two-pass EBU R128 normalization at I=-20, TP=-1.5, LRA=11, applied
per line before assembly.

**v3 helped on its own**, arriving at 3.4 LU where turbo gave 8.2. Normalization
took it the rest of the way.

## What v3 does and does not support

Probed rather than assumed.

| parameter | result |
|---|---|
| `model_id: eleven_v3` | works |
| `seed` | **honoured and reproducible**, 3.440 s twice on the same seed |
| `stability`, `similarity_boost` | accepted |
| **`speed`** | **silently ignored**. 3.76 s at 1.0, 0.9, 0.8 and 0.7, identical to the millisecond |

That last one is the consequence to know about. `eleven_turbo_v2_5` at speed 0.9
gave 157.5 wpm, which is the pace this film was fitted to. **v3 has no rate
control and reads at about 177 wpm.**

## The cost, stated plainly

Faster delivery against a fixed picture means more silence.

| act | silence, turbo build | silence, v3 build |
|---|---|---|
| Act 1 | 16.1% | 24.7% |
| Act 2 | 19.5% | 23.1% |
| Act 3 | 24.3% | 30.8% |
| Act 4 | 18.1% | 27.2% |
| Act 5 | 24.5% | 29.7% |

All inside the 10 to 35% the validator permits, but above the 15 to 20% target,
and `assemble_voice` flags acts 4 and 5 explicitly: "OVER 25%. Set wpm to about
176 in the spec and write more, not shorter."

**Acted on 2026-09-17**, and it exposed a worse defect first. See below.

## A defect I introduced, found by review, not by me

Review reported lines missing and the timing off around the partitioning passage.
Nothing was missing. **Act 2 lines 5 and 6 were running together with a 0.05
second pause**, which is no pause at all, because when I cleared an overrun
earlier I nudged line 6 to 33.44 s and destroyed the gap instead of shortening the
line. A second boundary, Act 5 lines 4 to 5, had the same fault at 0.12 s.

**The check that would have caught it did not exist.** I had been verifying that
no line *overruns* the next, which both boundaries passed, and never that a real
pause survives between them. An audit of all 31 boundaries now runs, requiring at
least 0.30 s, and a second check confirms every line is audibly present at its
intended time in the assembled act rather than assuming the assembler placed it.

Both are fixed by shortening the offending lines, which is what should have
happened the first time. Every boundary now clears 0.30 s, minimum 0.34 s.

## Words added, to capacity rather than to target

Per-line capacity was computed from each act's own measured rate, leaving a 0.40 s
pause, and the lines written to it. Several first-pass additions did not fit and
were reverted: the calculation is the check, not the intention.

| act | words | silence | min gap | density |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 | 90 | 22.0% | 0.35 s | 137.1 |
| Act 2 | 90 | 24.0% | 0.34 s | 129.5 |
| Act 3 | 81 | 28.6% | 0.37 s | 131.0 |
| Act 4 | 110 | 26.4% | 0.96 s | 129.4 |
| Act 5 | 81 | 29.7% | 0.52 s | 131.0 |

452 words, up from 444. **Far short of the 60 that was asked for**, and the reason
is structural: the beat gaps cap how much any line can carry, so with the picture
fixed and v3 reading at about 177 wpm, silence cannot be driven back into the 15
to 20% band by writing alone. Acts 3, 4 and 5 still sit above 25%.

Closing that properly needs the picture shortened, not the script lengthened,
which is a re-render of those three acts and a decision about whether the extra
silence is worth it.

## What I cannot check

Whether the enunciation variation is gone. I measured loudness and I measured
rate; **timbre and emphasis I cannot hear.** If v3 still reads unevenly, the
remaining levers are a different voice, or v3's audio-tag markup, which lets a
line carry an explicit delivery instruction. Both are worth trying before
concluding the voice is wrong.
